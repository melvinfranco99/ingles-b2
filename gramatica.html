Esta es toda la gramática que se evalúa en el B2, sobre todo en las Partes 2 y 4 del Use of English, pero también en Writing y Speaking (donde usar estructuras variadas sube la nota de Language). Cada tema tiene explicación en español, ejemplos y un ejercicio con corrección y explicación.

1. Presentes y verbos de estado

FormaUsoEjemplo
Present simpleHábitos, verdades generales, horariosShe works in Madrid. The train leaves at 8.
Present continuousAhora mismo, situaciones temporales, cambios en progreso, planes fijados, quejas con alwaysI’m staying with friends this week. Prices are rising. He’s always losing his keys!

Verbos de estado (normalmente NO van en continuo): know, believe, understand, mean, like, love, hate, prefer, want, need, belong, own, seem, contain, consist.

Algunos cambian de significado: I think it’s great (opino) / I’m thinking about it (lo estoy considerando). She has a car (posee) / She’s having lunch (acción). This soup tastes good / The chef is tasting the soup. You look tired / I’m looking for my phone.

2. Pasados · used to / would / be used to

FormaUsoEjemplo
Past simpleAcción terminada en un momento concretoI met him in 2019.
Past continuousAcción en progreso interrumpida o de fondoI was cooking when the phone rang.
Past perfectAcción anterior a otra pasadaWhen we arrived, the film had already started.
Past perfect continuousDuración antes de un momento pasadoShe was tired because she had been running.
EstructuraSignificadoEjemplo
used to + infinitivoHábito o estado pasado que ya no ocurreI used to live in Seville. Did you use to play?
would + infinitivoHábito pasado repetido (solo acciones, no estados)Every summer we would go to the beach.
be used to + -ing / nombreEstar acostumbradoI’m used to getting up early.
get used to + -ing / nombreAcostumbrarse (proceso)You’ll soon get used to driving on the left.
❌ I would live in Seville (estado) → ✅ I used to live in Seville.
❌ I’m used to get up → ✅ I’m used to getting up.

3. Present perfect simple y continuous

Conecta el pasado con el presente. En español a veces se traduce por presente (llevo dos años aquí = I’ve been here for two years).

  • Simple: resultado o cantidad: I’ve written three emails. Experiencias: Have you ever been to Japan? Con just, already, yet, ever, never, so far, recently.
  • Continuous: énfasis en la duración o actividad: I’ve been writing emails all morning. Your eyes are red. Have you been crying?
  • for + periodo (for ten years) · since + punto de inicio (since 2015, since I was a child).
  • It’s the first time I’ve + participio: It’s the first time I’ve eaten sushi.
  • Transformación típica: I started working here five years ago → I have been working here for five years. The last time I saw him was in May → I haven’t seen him since May.

4. El futuro

FormaUsoEjemplo
willDecisiones espontáneas, predicciones (opinión), promesas, ofertasI’ll help you. I think it will rain.
be going toPlanes/intenciones; predicción con evidenciaI’m going to study medicine. Look at those clouds: it’s going to rain.
Present continuousPlanes concertados (agenda)I’m meeting Ana at six.
Present simpleHorarios; tras when, as soon as, until, before, after, ifI’ll call you when I arrive (❌ when I will arrive).
Future continuousAcción en progreso en un momento futuroThis time tomorrow I’ll be lying on the beach.
Future perfectAcción terminada antes de un momento futuroBy June I’ll have finished the course.
be about to / be due to / be likely toInminente / previsto / probableThe film is about to start. He is likely to win.

5. Condicionales

TipoEstructuraUsoEjemplo
0If + present, presentVerdades generalesIf you heat ice, it melts.
1If + present, will + inf.Posible en el futuroIf it rains, we’ll stay in.
2If + past, would + inf.Hipotético / irreal presenteIf I had more time, I would travel. If I were you, I’d accept.
3If + past perfect, would have + part.Irreal pasadoIf I had studied, I would have passed.
MixtoIf + past perfect, would + inf.Pasado → presenteIf I had taken the job, I would be rich now.

Alternativas a if (muy frecuentes en la Parte 4):

  • unless = if… not: You won’t pass unless you study.
  • provided (that) / providing / as long as / on condition that = solo si: You can go as long as you’re back by ten.
  • in case = por si acaso (¡no es «if»!): Take an umbrella in case it rains.
  • otherwise = si no: Hurry, otherwise we’ll be late.
  • But for / Without + nombre: But for your help, I would have failed.
  • Inversión formal: Had I known, I would have come (= If I had known). Should you need help, call me.

6. Wish, if only, would rather, it’s time

EstructuraSignificadoEjemplo
wish / if only + past simpleDeseo sobre el presenteI wish I had a car. (no lo tengo)
wish / if only + past perfectArrepentimiento sobre el pasadoI wish I had listened to you.
wish + wouldQueja sobre lo que otro hace (o no hace)I wish you would stop shouting.
wish + couldHabilidad deseadaI wish I could swim.
would rather + inf.Preferencia propiaI’d rather stay home.
would rather + sujeto + pastPreferencia sobre otroI’d rather you didn’t smoke here.
It’s (high) time + sujeto + pastYa va siendo horaIt’s time we left.

Transformación típica: I regret not going to the party → I wish I had gone to the party.

7. La pasiva y el causativo

Pasiva = be (en el tiempo del verbo activo) + participio. El agente (by…) solo se pone si importa.

TiempoActivaPasiva
Present simpleThey clean the room.The room is cleaned.
Present continuousThey are cleaning it.It is being cleaned.
Present perfectThey have cleaned it.It has been cleaned.
Past simpleThey cleaned it.It was cleaned.
Future / modalThey will / must clean it.It will / must be cleaned.
Modal perfectoThey should have cleaned it.It should have been cleaned.
  • Pasiva impersonal: People say that he is rich → It is said that he is rich / He is said to be rich. Pasado: He is believed to have left the country.
  • Causativo (que alguien haga algo por ti): have/get + objeto + participio: I had my hair cut. We’re getting the car repaired. También para cosas malas: She had her bag stolen.
  • Verbos con dos objetos: They gave me a prize → I was given a prize.

8. Estilo indirecto y verbos de reporte

Al contar lo que alguien dijo con un verbo en pasado, los tiempos «retroceden» un paso:

DirectoIndirecto
“I am tired.”She said (that) she was tired.
“I have finished.”He said he had finished.
“I saw it.”He said he had seen it.
“I will call.”She said she would call.
“I can help.”He said he could help.
here / today / tomorrow / yesterday / agothere / that day / the next day / the day before / before

Preguntas: orden de frase afirmativa y sin do: “Where do you live?” → He asked me where I lived. “Are you coming?” → She asked if/whether I was coming.

Verbos de reporte (Parte 4 los adora) — fíjate en la estructura:

EstructuraVerbosEjemplo
verbo + to-inf.agree, offer, promise, refuse, threaten, decideHe refused to help.
verbo + objeto + to-inf.advise, ask, encourage, invite, remind, warn, persuade, tellShe reminded me to lock the door.
verbo + -ingadmit, deny, suggest, recommend, regretHe denied taking the money. She suggested going by train.
verbo + prep. + -ingapologise for, accuse sb of, blame sb for, insist on, congratulate sb on, object toThey accused him of lying. He apologised for being late.
suggest + that + sujeto + (should) + inf.suggest, recommend, insistShe suggested that we (should) leave. ❌ suggested us to leave

9. Verbos modales

Obligación y necesidad

  • must (obligación que siente el hablante) / have to (obligación externa): I must call Mum. I have to wear a uniform.
  • mustn’t = prohibido · don’t have to / needn’t = no es necesario. ¡Diferencia clave!
  • should / ought to = consejo. had better + inf. = más vale que: You’d better hurry.
  • be supposed to = se supone que (norma o expectativa): You’re supposed to wear a helmet.
  • be allowed to / be permitted to = tener permiso.

Deducción (presente y pasado)

CertezaPresentePasado
Seguro que síHe must be at home.He must have left early.
PosibleShe may / might / could be ill.She may / might / could have missed the bus.
Seguro que noIt can’t be true.He can’t / couldn’t have seen us.

Modales en pasado

  • should have + part. = debería haber (y no lo hice): I should have studied more.
  • needn’t have + part. = lo hice pero no era necesario: You needn’t have brought a present.
  • didn’t need to + inf. = no era necesario (normalmente no lo hice).
  • could (habilidad general) vs was able to / managed to (lo conseguí en una ocasión): Despite the fog, we managed to find the way.

10. Oraciones de relativo

  • who (personas) · which (cosas) · that (ambas, solo en especificativas) · whose (posesión) · where (lugar) · when (tiempo) · why (razón, tras the reason).
  • Especificativas (defining): identifican; sin comas; se puede omitir el pronombre si es objeto: The book (that) I read was great.
  • Explicativas (non-defining): información extra; con comas; nunca that y nunca se omite: My brother, who lives in Bilbao, is a doctor.
  • which puede referirse a toda la frase anterior: He passed, which surprised everyone.
  • Con preposición (formal): the person to whom I spoke = the person (who) I spoke to.
  • Cuantificadores: I have two brothers, both of whom are older. He bought ten books, most of which were novels.

11. Gerundio (-ing) o infinitivo (to)

Tras…UsaEjemplos
enjoy, avoid, mind, finish, suggest, consider, admit, deny, miss, risk, practise, can’t stand, can’t help, it’s worth, it’s no use, look forward to, be used to, object to-ingI don’t mind waiting. It’s worth visiting. I look forward to hearing from you.
Preposiciones (siempre)-ingShe’s good at drawing. Thanks for coming. Instead of complaining…
want, decide, hope, expect, plan, agree, refuse, manage, afford, seem, promise, offer, learn, tend, failto + inf.We can’t afford to buy it. He failed to arrive.
make, let, help (+ objeto); modales; had better; would ratherinf. sin toThey made me wait. Let him go. (Pasiva: I was made to wait.)

Cambian de significado:

  • remember / forget + -ing = recordar algo hecho · + to = acordarse de hacer: I remember meeting her. Remember to call her.
  • stop + -ing = dejar de · + to = parar para: He stopped smoking. He stopped to smoke.
  • try + -ing = probar (experimento) · + to = intentar (esfuerzo).
  • regret + -ing = arrepentirse · regret to inform = lamentar comunicar.
  • mean + -ing = implicar · mean to = tener intención.
  • go on + -ing = seguir haciendo · go on to = pasar a hacer después.

12. Comparativos y superlativos

  • Cortos: cheap → cheaper → the cheapest; largos: more / the most expensive. Irregulares: good/better/best, bad/worse/worst, far/further/furthest.
  • Modificadores: much / far / a lot / considerably (mucho) · slightly / a bit (un poco): far better, slightly cheaper.
  • (not) as … as: It isn’t as cold as yesterday. not such a … as.
  • The + comparativo, the + comparativo: The more you practise, the better you get.
  • Progresivo: It’s getting colder and colder. more and more people.
  • the same as, different from, similar to. Superlativo + present perfect: It’s the best film I’ve ever seen = I’ve never seen such a good film.

13. Artículos y cuantificadores

  • a/an: mencionado por primera vez, profesiones (She’s an engineer). the: algo concreto o único, ya mencionado, superlativos, instrumentos (play the piano), ríos, mares, cordilleras, the UK/the USA.
  • Sin artículo: generalizaciones con plurales e incontables (Dogs are loyal. Life is short), comidas, deportes, la mayoría de países y ciudades, at home, at work, go to bed, by car.
  • few / a few (contables) · little / a little (incontables): sin a = poco (negativo); con a = algo (positivo): I have a few friends here (algunos) / He has few friends (casi ninguno).
  • Incontables (sin plural ni a): advice, information, news, furniture, equipment, luggage, research, knowledge, accommodation, homework, progress, traffic, weather. ❌ an advice → ✅ a piece of advice.
  • each (uno a uno) / every (todos, 3+) · both (los dos) / either (cualquiera de los dos) / neither (ninguno de los dos) · none of · all / whole: all the day ❌ → the whole day / all day.

14. so/such, too/enough, as/like

  • so + adjetivo/adverbio: It was so cold (that) we stayed in. · so much/many/few/little.
  • such (a/an) + (adjetivo +) nombre: It was such a cold day. such nice people.
  • too + adj. (+ for sb) + to-inf.: He’s too young to drive. · adj. + enough / enough + nombre: not old enough, enough money.
  • like + nombre (= parecido a): She sings like an angel. · as + nombre (= en calidad de): He works as a waiter. · as + frase: As I said… · as if / as though: He looks as if he’s tired.
  • Contraste: although / even though / though + frase · despite / in spite of + nombre/-ing · however (inicio de frase, con coma) · whereas / while (contraste entre dos cosas): Despite feeling ill, she went. = Although she felt ill… · In spite of the fact that…
  • Finalidad: to / in order to / so as to + inf. · so that + frase (con can/could/would) · for + nombre.

15. Preguntas indirectas y question tags

  • Indirectas (más educadas): Could you tell me where the station is? Do you know what time it starts? I wonder if you could help me. → orden afirmativo, sin do/does/did.
  • Question tags: afirmativa → tag negativa y viceversa, con el mismo auxiliar: You’re Spanish, aren’t you? She didn’t call, did she? I’m late, aren’t I? Let’s go, shall we?
  • Muy útiles en Speaking Parte 3 para implicar al compañero: That’s a good idea, isn’t it?

16. Inversión y frases enfáticas (subir nota)

Tras ciertas expresiones negativas al inicio de la frase, se invierte sujeto y auxiliar (como en una pregunta):

  • Never have I seen such chaos. · Rarely / Seldom do we eat out.
  • Not only did he arrive late, but he also forgot the tickets.
  • Hardly / Scarcely had I sat down when the phone rang. · No sooner had I sat down than…
  • Under no circumstances should you open this door. · Not until I got home did I realise.
  • Only when / Only after I read it did I understand.

Cleft sentences (énfasis): What I need is a holiday. It was Sarah who told me. All I want is some peace.
